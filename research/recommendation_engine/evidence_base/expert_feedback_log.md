## 1. Review Context & Purpose

The initial MathQuest rule draft (*MathQuest | Expert Review Pack*, October 2026) formulated eight preliminary pedagogical and safeguard rules (R1–R8). The primary purpose of the consultation was to review:
1. Plausibility and developmental appropriateness for Sri Lankan primary school children (aged 6–10).
2. Safeguards against premature or biased labeling of children.
3. Separation of educational support decisions from medical ADHD diagnosis.

## 2. Rule-by-Rule Verbal Review Outcomes

| Rule ID | Original Proposed Rule Statement | Reported Expert Decision | Expert Verbal Feedback & Requested Changes | Current Validation & Implementation Status |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **R1** | *Do not label a mathematics concept as mastered from one correct response. Look for performance across multiple comparable opportunities.* | **REJECTED** | **Verbal Feedback:** The expert rejected R1 in its current formulation as an overarching rule. Mastery determinations in primary education require a nuanced balance: demanding too many identical repetitions can frustrate or disengage an inattentive learner, whereas single-item success is inadequate. A rigid repetition count without specifying task equivalence or scaffolding fade is pedagogically and clinically flawed.<br><br>**Pending Clarification:** What exact minimum observation threshold or adaptive criteria should govern provisional competence without inducing task fatigue? | **Draft Rejected.** Needs conceptual redesign into an adaptive evidence-accumulation model (e.g., Bayesian Beta-Binomial with uncertainty bounds) rather than a fixed-count hurdle. |
| **R2** | *When a child struggles repeatedly with a concept, suggest an easier worked example or a smaller step rather than simply repeating the same item.* | **ACCEPTED** | **Verbal Feedback:** Accepted without objection. Supported by clinical and developmental principles: repeated identical failure triggers frustration, anxiety, and task avoidance. Offering a worked example or breaking the problem down into smaller "mini-tasks" aligns directly with executive functioning interventions (supported by Boston Children's Hospital Skill 6 and IES Recommendation 1 / Recommendation 5). | **Accepted in Principle.** Awaiting translation into concrete scaffolding ladder triggers (Level 1 $\rightarrow$ Level 2 $\rightarrow$ Level 3). |
| **R3** | *If the child succeeds after a hint, record supported success separately from independent success; do not automatically treat them as the same.* | **ACCEPTED** | **Verbal Feedback:** Accepted. Essential for developmental validity. Conflating assisted success with unassisted competence misrepresents the child's independent mathematical mastery and Zone of Proximal Development. Supported by educational measurement principles and foundational to the separation of IMPSA and IMLR. | **Accepted in Principle.** Implemented in Milestone 1 data contract via `isFirstResponseCorrect` vs. assisted `userAnswer`. |
| **R4** | *After supported success, offer a comparable unassisted opportunity later before inferring independent performance.* | **REVISION REQUIRED** | **Verbal Feedback:** The core logic is sound, but the reassessment timing must be **flexible rather than fixed**. A child should not be forced into an immediate rigid retest, nor should the delay be so long that working memory limits prevent concept consolidation. Timing must adapt to session flow, child fatigue, and curriculum sequencing.<br><br>**Pending Clarification:** What flexible interval (immediate transfer probe vs. interleaved task vs. next-session probe) is optimal for primary school children with attention difficulties? | **Revision Required.** Requires formal definition of flexible transfer intervals within the dynamic assessment protocol. |
| **R5** | *If responses are unusually slow or incomplete, do NOT infer inattention or ADHD; allow for reading, motor, device, language, fatigue and task difficulty factors.* | **ACCEPTED WITH CLARIFICATION** | **Verbal Feedback:** Strongly accepted as a critical ethical and clinical safeguard, with specific clinical clarification: While slow response latency or incomplete work *may* indeed be associated with attention difficulties, **it must never be treated as definitive evidence of inattention or ADHD**. Alternative confounding explanations must be systematically ruled out or captured, including:<br>• Second-language or bilingual reading comprehension (Sinhala vs. English)<br>• Device interaction barriers (touchscreen precision, mouse familiarity)<br>• Physical/sensory states: hunger, tiredness, sleep disruption<br>• Sensory processing or motor coordination issues<br>• Inherent task difficulty and complexity | **Accepted with Clarification.** Serves as the primary non-penalizing safeguard in the recommendation engine. Fully aligned with AAP 2019 KAS 2/3 and NICE NG87 Section 1.3. |
| **R6** | *If repeated attempts show frustration or disengagement signals, offer an optional break/help/shorter activity without penalty.* | **REVISION REQUIRED** | **Verbal Feedback:** The intention (preventing emotional distress and cognitive overload) is vital, but the rule requires revision because **the exact criteria for detecting "frustration" and "disengagement" need clarification** to avoid high false-positive rates or patronizing interruptions.<br><br>**Pending Clarification:** What objective, non-intrusive game observables (e.g., rapid error cycling, prolonged idle timeout $>45\text{s}$, repetitive random tapping) reliably trigger a gentle break prompt without frustrating a child who is merely thinking deeply? | **Revision Required.** Trigger thresholds and intervention modes (gentle avatar check-in vs. break card) must be refined and agreed upon. |
| **R7** | *Where evidence is sparse, inconsistent or disrupted, report "insufficient evidence" rather than a fixed learner category.* | **ACCEPTED** | **Verbal Feedback:** Accepted. Ethically essential. Prevents diagnostic or educational overclaiming. Incomplete data must always trigger an explicit uncertainty advisory (`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`) rather than assigning a default deficit or false mastery label. Supported by psychometric measurement ethics and AAP/NICE clinical boundaries. | **Accepted in Principle.** Implemented in data contracts via `status: "INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE"` and `evidenceSufficiency` flags. |
| **R8** | *Describe parent/teacher outputs as observable patterns and suggested support, not clinical traits or fixed ability labels.* | **ACCEPTED** | **Verbal Feedback:** Strongly accepted. Adult-facing outputs must strictly avoid clinical or stigmatizing diagnostic language (e.g., never say "child exhibits severe attention deficit"). Instead, use positive, actionable educational guidance (e.g., "The learner worked best when visual counters were provided; consider using pictorial aids before symbolic equations"). | **Accepted in Principle.** Governs the UI text generation and explainability reason-code generation in the profile service. |

## 3. Methodological Note & Validation Scope

- **Preliminary Nature:** This record documents **user-reported verbal expert consultation**, not formal written psychometric or clinical validation.
- **Protocol Standards:** Per the *Expert Review Pack* (Section E), a formal expert-validation claim requires documented item-level judgments, standardized revision rubrics, and a defined evaluation protocol involving multiple independent reviewers.
- **Scientific Boundary:** No algorithm, cutoff score, or classification model in MathQuest may claim clinical validation based solely on this preliminary discussion.
