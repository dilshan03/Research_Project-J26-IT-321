# 1. Assessment Overview (plain language)

> DRAFT FOR EXPERT REVIEW. This describes a proposed research assessment that has not been validated.

## What MathQuest is trying to understand

MathQuest wants to give each Grade 1 child mathematics activities that suit them. To do that it needs two different
kinds of evidence, which are easy to confuse.

### IMPSA: what the child can do on their own, right now
**Independent Mathematical Problem-Solving Ability.** It is the child's answers to suitable questions **before** any
hint, demonstration or "right/wrong" feedback.

*Example:* the child is shown three groups of birds and, without any help, taps the group with the most.

### IMLR: what the child can do on their own after a short teaching step
**Independent Mathematical Learning Readiness.** After a brief, standard teaching activity, can the child solve **new,
similar** questions **without help**?

*Example:* after being shown how to count and compare groups of rabbits and balls, the child is given new groups of
elephants, butterflies and flowers and taps the group with the most, with no help.

### Why they must be kept apart
- A correct answer given **with** help shows what the child can do with support. It is not independent ability.
- A child who already knew the answer before teaching shows existing knowledge, not a response to teaching.
- A child who gets one new question right may simply have guessed (with three choices, about 1 in 3).

## What the proposed assessment does

1. **Two questions before teaching**, answered alone (help only if the child asks).
2. **Two guided teaching questions** that **every** child receives, with standard, step-by-step support.
3. **An evidence boundary.** The teaching ends, the support pictures are removed, and everything recorded so far is
   frozen.
4. **Three new questions**, never seen before, of similar difficulty, answered alone (help only if the child asks).

Only answers where the record **confirms** that no help was given before the first answer count as independent.
If the record is missing or unclear, the answer is marked **unknown**, never assumed to be independent.

## What it can and cannot show

| It **can** describe | It **cannot** show |
|---|---|
| How many of the new questions the child answered correctly without help, e.g. "2 of 3", next to the guessing level | That the teaching **caused** any improvement |
| Whether help, breaks or missing records affected an answer | That the child has permanently learned the idea |
| How much support the child used during teaching | A learning-readiness "score", level or category; no thresholds are used |
| | Anything about attention, ADHD or any other condition. The assessment makes no diagnostic or clinical claims and does not use ADHD or SNAP-IV information. |

Three questions are **not** a validated measure of learning readiness. The results are descriptive evidence for
teachers and researchers to discuss, pending the reviews in this package.

## Where this sits in MathQuest

The assessment is a **research prototype only**. It is not part of the live onboarding that children currently use.
Any future use would need the approvals listed in [08_decision_log.md](08_decision_log.md) and
